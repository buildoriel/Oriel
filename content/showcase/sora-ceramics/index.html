From wheel to kiln

Eleven days, four hands,
one fire.

Aya Nakamura-Reis apprenticed for six years in Kyoto’s Gojōzaka before opening Sora in a former bakery in Lisbon’s Marvila. Every piece still passes through her hands, or Tomás’s, at each of these steps.

  1. 01

    Throw

    Local stoneware is wedged, centred and pulled up on a kick wheel, about 600 g for a cup, 4 kg for a moon jar.

  2. 02

    Trim & dry

    Leather-hard pieces are turned upside down and trimmed to reveal the foot, then dried slowly under cloth for a week.

  3. 03

    Glaze

    Each pot is dipped by hand. Tide is poured twice, so the glaze runs thick and pools where the walls curve.

  4. 04

    Fire

    A 26-hour gas reduction firing to 1280°C. We open the kiln together, keep about four in five, and sign each foot.

Care notes

Dishwasher and microwave safe. Unglazed feet can absorb water; let them dry fully before stacking. Avoid sudden temperature changes such as a hot oven to a cold sink. Small iron spots and pinholes are part of the clay, not flaws.
